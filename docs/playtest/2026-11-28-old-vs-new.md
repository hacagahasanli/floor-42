Tester: game-playtest-critic (AI), playing as an impatient first-time CrazyGames player.
Builds tested:
- **OLD**: `ab70691`, built with `npm run build:portal` in a temporary worktree (600.6 KB gzip). This is the commit the 27 Nov zip came from. I did not byte-compare it with the uploaded zip.
- **NEW**: `portal-build` at **`fce5403`** (781.0 KB gzip), built in a separate detached worktree.

Player: a **scripted masher**, identical for both builds. It reads the menu for 2.5 s, clicks PLAY, then loops: alternate A/D every ~1.1 s, Space every 140 ms, W every ~0.4 s. This stands in for a newcomer who has not learned anything yet. **It is not a human.** Read its win rates as a *relative* signal between the builds, not as real balance numbers.

Evidence folder: `docs/playtest/2026-11-28-old-vs-new/`. It has `shots/`, `sheets/`, `video/` and `json/` (raw timelines), plus `scripts/`, which re-runs everything: `session.mjs`, `normal.mjs`, `twop.mjs`, `sheet.py` and `videos.sh`.

---

## Verdict (3 lines)
1. **NEW is the better game for a CrazyGames stranger.** In NEW a newcomer scores within 2–6 s of FIGHT! and wins the first match. In OLD the same masher went 0–5 and **never scored in the first 60 s, in 0 of 2 runs**. NEW also looks like a game on the thumbnail and fits 907x510 cleanly.
2. **It is not better on every axis.** OLD has a clearer KO moment (a big centred "X IS OUT" banner), an honest result screen and less HUD clutter, and it ran with no long frames. NEW over-corrected the bot: the *normal* bot lost 2 of 3 matches to a key-masher. NEW's result screen hands its awards to the **loser**.
3. Treat all of this as opinion until the CrazyGames numbers come in. Snapshot OLD's dashboard **today, before the update**, and compare 7 like-for-like days (§7). Expect only large effects to be detectable at Basic Launch traffic.

## 5. Against what top CrazyGames 2-player shooters do in minute one
[Assumption: from general knowledge of games like Rooftop Snipers, Getaway Shootout and Ragdoll Archers. **I did not replay them today**, so this is not timed evidence.]
- **Fight within about 5 s of load, with no menu reading.** Both our builds take about 5.3–5.7 s with a quick click. That is at the bar. Humans will be slower on NEW's richer home screen.
- **One or two keys per player, learned by pressing them.** We show 7 actions (A/D/W/Space/Shift/Q/F) in the first strip. That is more than the leaders, but auto-aim means mashing Space works in NEW.
- **Rounds of seconds, with the round as the unit of fun.** A whole leader match is often under a minute. NEW's 42–60 s first-to-5 is close. OLD's 56–83 s losing streak is not.
- **Physics comedy is the hit feel.** Ragdolls and knock-off falls deliver the laugh on every KO. Neither build has a comparable "lol" moment per KO; ours is a damage number plus a banner or chip. This is our biggest gap to the leaders in both builds.
- **Instant rematch with almost no screen between rounds.** Both builds take about 2.3 s from REMATCH to fight, plus a result screen per match. NEW's result screen is pretty but long to read.

## 6. Top 5 changes that would most raise NEW's playtime (ranked by impact on "would a stranger keep playing?")
1. **Fix the difficulty curve after match 1.** Keep match 1 winnable. Make "normal" beat the masher at least 70% of the time [Estimate target], measured with this repo's `scripts/normal.mjs`. After a player win, the result screen should offer "HARDER BOT?". After a loss, step down quietly. Evidence: R1. Systems designer to set the numbers.
2. **Make the result screen reward the player.** At least one award always goes to YOU when you win. Show the bot's awards only when it won. Show "so close!" only when the margin is 1. Evidence: R2, R3. This is cheap and hits the rematch moment directly.
3. **Make the KO moment unmissable.** Put a centred, short KO stamp with your rival's name over the fight for about 0.6 s, as OLD did, plus a hit-stop or zoom punch. Prewarm whatever causes the 216–316 ms frames (R4, R5). If feasible, add one funny physical beat per KO (knock-back tumble). That is the leaders' hook.
4. **Give 1P a session loop.** Use 2P's "Tonight: P1 1–0 P2" tally in 1P as well (a win streak vs the bot), and push NEXT ARENA after a win ("3 arenas, beat them all"). Playtime is matches × about 50 s. The only lever beyond one match is a reason to play the next.
5. **Put the first minute on a text diet.** For first-time players, hide the trait jargon bubble on home. Hide the arena flavour ribbon in match 1. Show the respawn tip only from the 2nd death, and never over the key strip (R6, R7). The spare attention goes to the fight.

## 7. The real test that replaces opinion (CrazyGames dashboard)

Definitions from CrazyGames' Basic Launch docs ([source](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)):
- **Average play time:** average time per play. Benchmark: successful titles often 10+ min.
- **Day 1 retention:** new players who play again exactly 1 day later. Benchmark: strong games 10–15%. Shows one day late.
- **Conversion:** visits to the game page that lead to a play of **at least 1 minute**. Benchmark: top titles 80%+.
- **CTR:** thumbnail clicks per impression. Measured for games that start or restart Basic Launch on or after 23 Nov 2026.

The dashboard updates daily. Updates are auto-approved and show from the next refresh. Basic Launch ends after at least 7 days and 500 plays, or after 21 days.

**Before uploading NEW (today):**
1. Screenshot or export the dashboard for **every day OLD has been live**. Record per day: plays (gameplays), visits, conversion, average play time, D1 retention (where shown), CTR, impressions, and the Basic Launch day count. Save to `docs/playtest/2026-11-28-old-vs-new/dashboard/old-<date>.png` and a CSV.
2. Write down the exact upload time of NEW. Do not change the covers, title and game on different days. Better: change them together, and accept that **CTR then measures the new thumbnail and name, not the game**. Conversion, playtime and D1 measure the game.

**After uploading:**
3. Exclude the update day, which has mixed traffic. Collect **the same number of days as the OLD window**, and at least 7, so each weekday appears once (weekend traffic differs [Assumption]).
4. Compare per metric with traffic weighting. Use the totals for conversion and D1 (successes = rate × visits or new players). Weight daily average playtime by plays.

**Sample size: what difference is detectable** (two-proportion test, α = 0.05, power 0.8; n is per period):
- Conversion 60% vs 70% (+10 pp): about **360 visits** per period. +5 pp needs about 1,450.
- D1 5% vs 8%: about **1,050 new players** per period. Basic Launch traffic will probably **not** reach that, so treat D1 as directional only.
- Average playtime +20%: about **400 plays** per period, assuming SD ≈ mean (skewed playtime) [Assumption]. The dashboard gives daily means, not per-player data, so the honest check is that **at least 6 of 7 NEW days sit above the OLD daily maximum** (a sign test, p ≈ 0.06 for 6/7, p ≈ 0.008 for 7/7).
- At about 500 plays per Basic Launch week [Assumption, the docs' minimum], only **large effects** show: conversion ±8–10 pp, playtime ±20–30%. Smaller changes are noise. Say "no detectable difference", not "same".

**Meaningful:**
- NEW wins if conversion is up at least 8 pp, or average playtime is up at least 20%, with the other not down by more than half that.
- NEW is a regression if average playtime is down at least 15% with conversion flat. That would point to R1/R2: fewer rematches.
- Plays per visit (rematches) is the cleanest signal for this change. If the dashboard shows gameplays and visits, compute it.

**Cheaper leading indicator (this week): 5-person playtest, within-subjects.** Each person plays both builds for 5 minutes each, with the order randomised (3 start OLD, 2 start NEW). Record the screen and face.
- Say only: "This is a game on a games website. Play as long as you'd like, stop whenever you'd normally stop." Then stay silent.
- Do not explain controls, do not name the builds, do not react to wins or losses, and do not ask "is it fun?" during play.
- Watch for: seconds to the first key press; the first time they say "which one am I?"; the first KO they notice (do they react?); whether they read the home bubble; where they look at the result screen; whether they press REMATCH unprompted; the minute they stop.
- After both builds, ask: "Which one would you play again tomorrow? Why?", "What was the worst moment?", and "Show me the moment you understood how to win."
- Success bar for NEW: at least 4 of 5 pick NEW, and at least 3 of 5 rematch unprompted in NEW.

**Analytics engineering should add.** The portal build has no stats (`NEXT_PUBLIC_STATS_URL` is undefined), so CrazyGames' four numbers are all we get. To see *why*, add these to the site build first (floor42/pewfield site), then the portal if CrazyGames allows external beacons [not verified]:
